When you book a Marbella boat rental, the standard package includes drinks and light snacks – but if you want a proper vegetarian meal onboard, you need to plan ahead. The Costa del Sol is blessed with incredible local produce, from sun-ripened tomatoes to artisanal cheeses. This guide covers what the charter provides, what to bring, and how to pack a vegetarian feast that works on the water.

We run a fleet of 29 boats from Puerto Banús, from the compact Dubhe (5 pax) to the superyacht Ferretti 94 (29m, 12 pax). Every charter includes a licensed skipper, fuel, insurance, and Spanish IVA – so you can focus on enjoying the day, not logistics.

What Food Is Included With Your Charter?

Every single boat in our fleet – from the Astondoa 40 (€749 for 2 hours) to the Mangusta 80 (€4,719 minimum 4 hours) – comes with the following included in the price:

  • Water, soft drinks, beer, white wine, and cava

  • Ice and cool boxes

These are vegetarian-friendly. You do not need to bring drinks. But if you want a proper meal – lunch, tapas, or a picnic – you need to bring your own food.

7 Vegetarian Food Ideas for Your Boat Trip

Here are seven practical, tasty options that travel well and are easy to eat on a moving boat. Spanish staples work brilliantly because they are designed for warm weather and simple preparation.

1. Spanish Tortilla (Tortilla de Patatas)

A thick slice of cold tortilla is the perfect boat food. It is filling, non-messy, and tastes better at room temperature. Buy one from a local deli or make it the night before.

2. Gazpacho or Salmorejo

Cold Andalusian soups are refreshing and hydrating. Pour into a thermos or a sealed bottle. Serve in small cups with chopped vegetables on the side.

3. Hummus with Crudités

Hummus keeps well in a cooler. Pair with carrot sticks, cucumber, and bell pepper strips. Avoid crumbly crackers – they become crumbs in the sea breeze.

4. Manchego Cheese with Membrillo

Spain’s classic pairing: firm sheep’s milk cheese with quince paste. No melting, no mess. Slice and serve on a wooden board.

5. Fresh Fruit Skewers

Watermelon, melon, grapes, and strawberries are hydrating and easy to eat. Skewer them for a no-fuss snack.

6. Vegetable Wraps

Fill tortilla wraps with roasted peppers, courgette, hummus, and rocket. Wrap tightly in foil – they survive the boat ride intact.

7. Crusty Bread with Olive Oil and Tomatoes

Pan con tomate: rub a cut tomato on toasted bread, drizzle with olive oil, and sprinkle with salt. Simple, authentic, and delicious.

Where to Buy Vegetarian Food Near Puerto Banús

You can stock up before departure. Here are the best spots within a 10-minute drive of Puerto Banús:

  • Mercadona (Calle Ribera, Puerto Banús) – good selection of fresh fruit, vegetables, cheese, and hummus.
  • Carrefour Express (Avenida del Puerto) – convenient for last-minute items, including pre-made salads.
  • Mercado Municipal de Marbella (Calle Virgen del Pilar) – a proper market with fresh produce, olives, and local cheese. Open mornings.
  • Estepona Saturday Market – excellent for organic vegetables and artisan bread (20 minutes from Puerto Banús).

For a special touch, visit a local deli (charcutería) and ask for vegetarian options – they often stock quince paste, marinated artichokes, and roasted peppers.

Packing Tips for a Boat Picnic

Boats have limited space and no kitchen. Here is how to pack smart:

  • Use a cooler bag – most boats have a fridge, but it is small. A cooler with ice packs keeps dips, cheese, and fruit cold.
  • Seal everything – sea spray and wind can ruin open containers. Use Tupperware with tight lids.
  • Bring reusable cups and plates – the boat provides glasses, but plastic or bamboo tableware is safer on deck.
  • Pack in portions – individual servings are easier to hand out than a big bowl.
  • Check with your skipper – some boats have a fridge and a sink; others only a cool box. Ask when you book.

What to Avoid Bringing

Some foods do not work on a boat. Avoid:

  • Melty cheese – brie and camembert become a mess in the sun.
  • Crumbly pastries – croissants and puff pastry create crumbs everywhere.
  • Mayonnaise-based salads – they spoil quickly without proper refrigeration.
  • Anything that needs reheating – there is no microwave or stove.
  • Glass bottles – many boats ban glass for safety. Use plastic or metal containers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I bring my own food on a Marbella boat rental?

Yes, you are welcome to bring your own food. There is no onboard kitchen, but most boats have a fridge and a small sink. Pack light, easy-to-eat meals that won't spoil in the heat. A cooler bag with ice packs is a good idea for dairy or dips.

What food is provided with the charter?

Every charter includes water, soft drinks, beer, white wine, cava, and light snacks (typically crisps, nuts, or olives). This is included in the price. You do not need to bring drinks, but you can supplement with your own if you prefer.

Are there vegetarian options available from the charter company?

The standard snacks provided are vegetarian-friendly (crisps, nuts, olives). For a full meal, you will need to bring your own. Some boats offer a catering upgrade with vegetarian options – ask when booking. Otherwise, local supermarkets and delis have excellent choices.

What are the best vegetarian foods to bring on a boat trip?

Ideal options are easy to eat, non-messy, and hold up in warm weather. Try Spanish tortilla (tortilla de patatas), gazpacho in a thermos, hummus with crudités, Manchego cheese with membrillo, fresh fruit, and crusty bread with olive oil. Avoid anything that melts or crumbles easily.

Where can I buy vegetarian food near Puerto Banús?

Puerto Banús has several supermarkets: Mercadona on Calle Ribera and Carrefour Express on Avenida del Puerto. For fresh produce, the Marbella market (Mercado Municipal de Marbella) is 10 minutes away. Estepona's Saturday market is also excellent for organic vegetables and local cheese.

Can I bring a cooler or esky on the boat?

Yes, you can bring a cooler bag. The boats have limited fridge space, so a small cooler with ice packs is practical for extra drinks or perishables. Check with your skipper before loading a large cooler – some boats have restricted deck space.

What vegetarian dishes travel well on a boat?

Wraps with roasted vegetables and hummus, quinoa salad with cucumber and tomato, Spanish tortilla slices, fruit skewers, and individual pots of gazpacho. Avoid creamy salads that spoil quickly or foods that need reheating. Pack everything in sealed containers to protect from sea spray.

Are there vegetarian-friendly restaurants near Marbella marinas?

Yes, many restaurants around Puerto Banús and Marbella Marina offer vegetarian options. Try La Pesquera for vegetable paella, or El Estrecho for grilled vegetables and salads. For a quick bite, the chiringuitos on the Golden Mile serve patatas bravas and pimientos de Padrón.
